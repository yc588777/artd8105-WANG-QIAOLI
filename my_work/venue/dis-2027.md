### Review criteria — verbatim

Papers CFP, “Reviewers will use below questions to assess submissions”:

> - Does the submission make a contribution to DIS/HCI communities (and beyond) and state its contribution clearly?
> - Is the submission well-situated, framed and well referenced within DIS and HCI especially, and outside of HCI where needed?
> - Are the implications for HCI and/or interaction design clear? These may be analytic, generative, synthesis-oriented, and even manifestos.

**Assisted Desk Reject** (verbatim criteria):

> - Scope: Grossly insufficient literature review to contextualize the proposed novelty/contribution to design research or interactive systems. … a paper making a disproportionately small contribution may be desk rejected, as the size of the contribution should be commensurate with the paper length.
> - Methodology: Grossly insufficient details provided to demonstrate research and methodological transparency and clarity…
> - Data: Grossly insufficient data to validate the analysis to support the claim.

Desk reject also includes: incomplete submissions; anonymisation breach; undeclared concurrent related submissions; wrong template; out of scope; not English; plagiarism; ACM AI-policy violations; citing non-existent literature.

A full numeric review *form* (scores per criterion) was **not** published on the 2027 pages opened on 2026-10-13. The three questions plus ADR criteria are what authors are told to write to.

Pictorials checklist (verbatim, condensed): contribution original and complete; images/diagrams emphasised over text as primary means; implications for the DIS research community clear; placement of images and text compose a coherent narrative. Expected acceptance rate “around 25%”.

### Contribution sentences, verbatim

From Bourgault & Jacobs 2024, end of Introduction:

> We contribute:
> (1) A methodology that supports the implementation of materialist theories in system development, using action as a bridging concept.
> (2) A design space for carved and CNC-milled surface texture highlighting a set of inherent components – movement, grid, and boundary – that reflect the theoretical and practical notion of action in the context of carving.
> (3) Millipath, a novel action-oriented system that enables the creation of surface texture on wood and other carvable materials by specifying low-level machine actions.
> (4) An analysis of autobiographical data collected during the creation of artifacts with Millipath, emphasizing how using action as a design tool influences human interactions with material and machine.

### What this venue appears to reward

From the CFP questions + this one exemplar (provisional):

- A contribution **named as such**, in DIS/HCI language, not only in computer-graphics language.
- Related work that builds a **gap**, including work outside HCI (here: craft and materialist theory).
- **Implications for interaction design** — analytic or generative, not just “we built a tool.”
- Making is evidenced with artifacts, toolpaths, and (in Millipath) autobiographical fabrication data — **not** a 6–10 person lab study. That is usable this term.

### What this venue appears to punish

- Pretty pictures with no HCI implication (student’s own literature report already flags this).
- Contribution too small for the length (ADR).
- Graphics-only papers that never cite DIS/HCI digital-craft work (Millipath’s session is exactly that literature).
- Empirical claims without ethics context.

### Width of the box — where the exemplars differ

Not yet knowable with one paper. DIS 2024–2026 proceedings include Digital Craft sessions **and** more-than-human / speculative sessions; the box is wider than fabrication systems. Fill B and C before session 10.
